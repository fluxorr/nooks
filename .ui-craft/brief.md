# Nooks — Design Brief

## 1. Product purpose

Your personal nook on the web — save anything, find everything, forget nothing.

## 2. Primary user

Anyone who saves links: a researcher bookmarking 20 papers a day, a student collecting references for a thesis, a designer stacking inspo, or someone who just wants to keep track of what they read. Session length is seconds for saves and seconds-to-minutes for finding. Works everywhere — extension, keyboard, web.

## 3. Principles

In conflict-resolution order:

1. **The link is the artifact.** Every UI decision serves saving, finding, and reading links. Chrome, animation, and decoration are secondary. If it doesn't help someone save or find a link faster, cut it.

2. **Save in one click, organize later.** Capture must be zero-friction. Never interrupt the save flow to ask for metadata. Extracting title, summary, and tags is the app's job, not the user's. The "Inbox" exists so nothing needs a home upfront.

3. **Search over browse.** Cmd+K is the primary navigation. Browsing collections is a secondary path. The user should never have to click through folders to find what they need.

4. **Personal library, not social feed.** This is a private collection. No timelines, no follows, no likes. Every design choice reinforces that the data belongs to the user alone.

## 4. Success metric

User opens the app, finds a specific link they saved three weeks ago within 10 seconds, and continues their day without friction.

## 5. Out of scope

- Does not support social features (sharing feeds, follows, likes, comments)
- Does not require an account or auth (P0)
- Does not edit or annotate the content at the saved URL — it's a link curator, not a document editor
- Does not track read/revisit status
- Does not sync with third-party bookmarking services (import/export is separate)
